Why does the acceleration of a body become zero at its highest point during vertical upward motion?
✓Verified explanation

Velocity is zero at the top. Acceleration is not.

At the highest point, the body's instantaneous velocity becomes zero for a moment before it begins to move downward. Gravity continues to act throughout the motion, so the acceleration remains directed downward with magnitude equal to g.

The key distinction is between velocity, which describes the current rate of motion, and acceleration, which describes how velocity changes.
